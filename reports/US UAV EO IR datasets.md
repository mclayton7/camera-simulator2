# Public data falls short; fly Pendleton yourself

No public dataset meets all of CamSim's sim-to-real requirements: US location, UAV or aircraft view at 50 m–3 km, labelled vehicles or vessels, EO plus thermal, an open-source-friendly licence, and per-frame telemetry. Every set that is US, aerial and thermal either has no vehicle labels (CSUAV, FLAME 3, WiSARD) or has only a few segmented cars and boats (Caltech CART). Every set with good aerial thermal vehicle boxes was captured abroad (HIT-UAV, DroneVehicle, MONET). The only US military-vehicle MWIR library, DSIAC's ATR database, was shot from the ground across a valley at 1–5 km, not from the air. The recommendation is therefore a three-tier test set. **First and primary**: a self-flown, self-labelled EO + LWIR collection over Camp Pendleton with DJI-class telemetry converted to MISB KLV. It is the only data that is geo-matched, pose-matched and licence-clean at once. **Second**: a public comparability tier: **VEDAI** (US, Utah aerial orthophotos, EO vehicles) and **HIT-UAV** (CC BY 4.0, 60–130 m LWIR vehicles with per-image altitude and angle), plus **SeaDronesSee** (CC0, with telemetry) for boats. **Third**: **Caltech CART**, the only US UAV RGB-thermal set with synchronised GPS/IMU. It lets CamSim replay the exact real flight path at night and compare pixels pose for pose, though it is only a light vehicle test. The owner's decisions centre on Pendleton flight and publication clearance, the target vehicle set to stage, and whether non-commercial benchmarks are acceptable alongside CamSim's open licence.

## No public set clears all six bars at once

The search covered academic EO benchmarks, US government and defence releases, aerial thermal and RGB-T sets, and maritime UAV sets. Each requirement removes a large share of candidates. **Geography** removes most of the popular UAV benchmarks. VisDrone, UAVDT, CARPK (Taiwan by inference), PUCPR+ (Curitiba, Brazil), UAV-OBB (Chongqing and Wuhan) and Songdo Traffic (South Korea) are all non-US ([LPN page](https://lafi.github.io/LPN/); [PKLot survey](https://www.ppgia.pucpr.br/~jean.barddal/assets/pdf/draft_pklot_survey2022.pdf); [UAV-OBB, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13092195/); [Songdo, arXiv 2411.02136](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02136v2)). DOTA's aerial portion is Dutch CycloMedia imagery plus Google Earth and Chinese satellites, with no coordinates in its metadata ([DOTA](https://captain-whu.github.io/DOTA/dataset.html)). **Platform** removes xView, which is 0.3 m satellite imagery ([xView](https://xviewdataset.org/)). It also removes the DSIAC ATR database, whose cameras sat on "slightly elevated positions overlooking a valley of dry scrubland" ([PMC10536622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10536622/)). **Labels** remove most of what is left. VIRAT Aerial was never annotated, because "annotating the aerial video proved extremely challenging" ([viratdata.org](https://viratdata.org/)). CSUAV's MWIR runs over Columbus carry no ground truth ([SDMS CSUAV](https://www.sdms.afrl.af.mil/index.php?collection=csuav)). The FHWA helicopter sets (TGSIM, HIGH-SIM) release trajectories rather than labelled frames ([data.gov TGSIM](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/third-generation-simulation-data-tgsim-i-294-l2-trajectories); [HIGH-SIM, DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c49abf4cd27f4beba34d1b152d654009)).

**Thermal** is the tightest bar. The only documented US airborne MWIR is CSUAV, a FLIR SC6000 at about 2.5 kft MSL over Ohio State in 2007 ([SDMS CSUAV](https://www.sdms.afrl.af.mil/index.php?collection=csuav)). Every open US UAV thermal set found is LWIR from Boson/Tau-class cores ([CART, arXiv 2403.08997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08997); [FLAME 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02831v1)). No public, labelled MISB-KLV FMV collection over the US turned up either. The well-known "Day Flight.mpg" and "Night Flight IR.mpg" STANAG 4609 clips sit on samples.ffmpeg.org with no licence or provenance ([FFmpeg samples](https://samples.ffmpeg.org/MPEG2/mpegts-klv/)). They are useful for KLV parser regression only, and their KLV must be extracted with `scripts/klv_conformance/check.js`, never ffmpeg, per CLAUDE.md. None of these searches proves a negative: two targeted searches for a 2022–2026 US UAV vehicle set with boxes, GPS and an open licence came up empty. University lab releases that are not indexed could exist.

## US EO candidates are orthophotos, campuses and highways

The US-located EO sets split into nadir orthophoto benchmarks and UAV sets over civilian scenes. **VEDAI** is built from "a modified version of the images from … UTAH AGRC … 2012 HRO 6-inch color orthophotography", available "in several spectral bands and resolutions" ([VEDAI terms](https://downloads.greyc.fr/vedai/TermsandConditionsofUseVeDAI2014.pdf); [VEDAI](https://downloads.greyc.fr/vedai/)), at **12.5 cm GSD for 1024² and 25 cm for 512²** ([arXiv 2307.06724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06724)). That look is close to NAIP, so CamSim scenes match its imagery well. A TUM thesis on simulation-trained vehicle detection names VEDAI and DOTA as the relevant real benchmarks ([TUM](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1721356/erncmqmyemcpiuubk811mgqcc.pdf)). Sim-to-real papers do not share a single yardstick, though. Most test on their own small real sets or on DOTA, VisDrone or VEDAI ([arXiv 2507.20976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20976)), so VEDAI is the best available rather than an agreed standard. Its weaknesses for CamSim are geometry and band: it is nadir only, with no oblique UAV views, no thermal and no coordinates in the chips. **COWC** adds Columbus and Utah, also at 15 cm ortho, but per prior knowledge its labels are car centre points, and its authors' competitors call its resolution low next to drone imagery ([COWC](https://gdo152.llnl.gov/cowc/); [arXiv 1707.05972](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05972)).

The UAV options over US ground are civilian and lightly labelled. **SDD** is true drone footage, about 80 m nadir over eight fixed Stanford scenes ([SDD](https://cvgl.stanford.edu/projects/uav_data/); [arXiv 2110.12638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.12638)). A scene package could be built over a hand-picked box, but cars, buses and carts are minority classes and there are no geotags. **CitySim** is stronger on paper: UAV video with rotated boxes, matching CamSim's OBB output, and positions "in pixels, feet, and GPS coordinates" ([arXiv 2208.11036](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11036)). Its US intersection sites are unnamed, though, and access needs a request form ([CitySim GitHub](https://github.com/UCF-SST-Lab/UCF-SST-CitySim1-Dataset); [tactics2d](https://tactics2d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dataset_support/citysim/)). **MEVA** stands out on licence and site. Its UAV video (4.6 h, two DJI Inspire 1 v2) was shot at the **Muscatatuck Urban Training Center**, a military training site in Indiana. It ships KRTD camera models, a 3D PLY site model and GPS logs under **CC BY 4.0** on AWS ([mevadata.org](https://mevadata.org/)). Its annotations are activity labels, however, so vehicle boxes would have to be added. The CC BY licence means those added labels could then be published with the frames.

The government WAMI sets (WPAFB 2009, CLIF 2007) are better treated as geo-registration testbeds than detector tests. They are mono EO at about 0.25 m GSD from an aircraft, so vehicles are a few pixels to a few tens of pixels long, and their Ohio scenes date from 2007–2009 ([SDMS WPAFB 2009](https://www.sdms.afrl.af.mil/index.php?collection=wpafb2009); [arXiv 1911.01727](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01727)). Today's NAIP will show changed buildings and roads, which spoils pixel-level comparison. The same date mismatch applies to CSUAV and VIVID.

## Aerial thermal over the US means CART, CSUAV, or nothing

**Caltech CART** is the one US UAV thermal set a CamSim scene package can reproduce exactly. It covers 18 aerial trajectories over Southern California, Kentucky and North Carolina, "mostly … from 40 m altitude", with 20° and 45° oblique cameras ([arXiv 2403.08997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08997); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08997v2)). The sensors are a FLIR ADK (Boson-based) LWIR at 640×512 and 75° HFoV, hardware-synchronised to RGB, a **VectorNav VN100 IMU at 200 Hz and a u-blox GPS at 5 Hz**, with raw Ardupilot logs and Kalibr calibrations ([arXiv 2403.08997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.08997); [GitHub](https://github.com/aerorobotics/caltech-aerial-rgbt-dataset)). That pose stream can drive CamSim over CIGI along the real path at sunrise, day and night. Over coastlines, lakes and desert, it would test CamSim's land-cover thermal classes, water crossover and sky directly. Its limits are significant. The thermal camera is **non-radiometric**. Vehicles and persons are "rare object classes" in segmentation masks, excluded from the paper's metrics. One third-party paper recovered only **223 paired samples with vehicle boxes** ([arXiv 2609.02556](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02556)). The README still lists "preferred position data" as "COMING SOON!". The 16-bit raw counts are an advantage, because CamSim's own AGC can be applied to the real data to put both domains on the same display curve.

**CSUAV** is the only documented **US airborne MWIR**, which matches CamSim's default `mwir_cooled` preset (640×512 InSb-class). It is a FLIR SC6000 at 30 Hz from about 2.5 kft MSL over Ohio State on 28 Oct 2007, with 2,087–3,723 IR frames per run across 15 runs, timestamped headers and known flight lines ([SDMS CSUAV](https://www.sdms.afrl.af.mil/index.php?collection=csuav)). It has no labels, and it was not confirmed that its headers carry GPS or attitude. It is a candidate for hand-labelling campus traffic, not a ready test set. **DARPA VIVID** has EO and IR of military and civilian vehicles from an aircraft at Eglin and Fort Pickett, but the clips are 2003-era and short, the waveband is unstated, and no platform telemetry is documented ([SDMS VSS#1](https://www.sdms.afrl.af.mil/index.php?collection=video_sample_set_1)). The PSU evaluation site returned HTTP 500 during this research.

The **DSIAC ATR Algorithm Development Image Database** is what an ATR audience will ask about. It holds visible and 16-bit MWIR (InSb, day and night) of Pickup, SUV, BTR70, BRDM2, BMP2, T72, ZSU23-4 and 2S3. Each target drives a 100 m circle at 1,000–5,000 m in 500 m steps, with met data and temperature differentials ([DSIAC](https://dsiac.dtic.mil/databases/atr-algorithm-development-image-database/); [PMC10536622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10536622/)). The near-horizontal ground geometry does not test a UAV detector. It could still serve as a secondary check on vehicle thermal signatures, which ROADMAP 4C models (engine, exhaust, running gear). CamSim can render that geometry with a low, distant camera, but it is not the UAV test this question asks for. Eglin's Signature Data Center holds the airborne equivalent, but it is CUI to classified and needs a government sponsor ([DSIAC TI report](https://dsiac.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/DSIAC-2190996.pdf)), so open-source use is out.

For absolute temperature, **FLAME 3** is the only confirmed radiometric aerial set. It provides per-pixel temperature TIFFs from DJI M30T, Mavic 2 EA and Autel 640T drones at 61–122 m over Arizona, Oregon and Florida burns ([arXiv 2412.02831](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02831v1)). It has no vehicles, and its licence is unstated, but it is the right real-data check on CamSim's LWIR terrain temperatures. That is a `thermal_check.py`-style test, not an ATR test. The strongest aerial IR vehicle benchmark is non-US. **HIT-UAV** has 2,898 LWIR images from a DJI M210 + XT2 at **60–130 m and 30–90°**, with altitude, angle and day/night encoded per image, Car/OtherVehicle classes with HBB and OBB, and a **CC BY 4.0** licence ([PMC10119175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10119175/); [GitHub](https://github.com/suojiashun/HIT-UAV-Infrared-Thermal-Dataset)). Its 8-bit AGC'd frames resemble CamSim's H.264 IR output after AGC, which keeps a detector comparison fair. DroneVehicle (28,439 RGB-IR pairs, oriented boxes) is larger, but its repo states no licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/VisDrone/DroneVehicle)). One mismatch runs through all of these: every open aerial set is LWIR, so CamSim should evaluate against them with its `lwir_uncooled` preset, not the default MWIR.

**Vessels** have no US UAV option. **SeaDronesSee** is CC0 ("Essentially, do what you want!"), with per-frame GPS, altitude, gimbal pitch and heading, 14,227 OD images at 5–260 m, and boat, jet-ski and buoy classes ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ben93kie/SeaDronesSee/main/README.md); [arXiv 2511.19728](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19728)). It was flown on Lake Constance, and its test labels are held behind an evaluation server. MassMIND is US (Boston Harbor) LWIR, but it was shot from a surface vessel, uses segmentation labels, and is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/uml-marine-robotics/MassMIND/main/LICENSE)).

## Licences sort into three tiers

The licence picture determines what CamSim can ship. **Clean for an open-source project**, meaning usable, publishable and redistributable with attribution, are MEVA (CC BY 4.0), VIRAT Aerial ("Distribution A, Release Unlimited"), HIT-UAV (CC BY 4.0 per its repo), SeaDronesSee (CC0) and WiSARD (MIT-style) ([mevadata.org](https://mevadata.org/); [viratdata.org](https://viratdata.org/); [WiSARD](https://sites.google.com/uw.edu/wisard/)). **Non-commercial research only** applies to VEDAI ("any use for commercial and / or industrial purpose is forbidden", and the licence must travel with every copy), SDD (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0), CART (non-commercial in both of its conflicting statements), xView, DOTA and CARPK ([VEDAI terms](https://downloads.greyc.fr/vedai/TermsandConditionsofUseVeDAI2014.pdf); [SDD](https://cvgl.stanford.edu/projects/uav_data/); [CaltechDATA](https://data.caltech.edu/records/cks6g-ps927)). CamSim can evaluate on these and publish metrics, but it should not commit the data or derived crops, bake them into the Docker image, or present them as usable by commercial ATR vendors. VEDAI's clause against using the data "to obtain any financial gain" is the sharpest. **Government-gated** covers DSIAC ATR, which is Distribution A but requested through a form, with DSIAC reserving "the right for final distribution" and one survey claiming NATO-only access ([DSIAC](https://dsiac.dtic.mil/databases/atr-algorithm-development-image-database/); [arXiv 2408.06868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.06868)). It also covers the SDMS collections (registration, no licence stated) and ESDC (CUI, excluded). Papers routinely publish results on DSIAC ATR, so metrics are safe. Raw frames are not.

The safe pattern for every non-clean set is the one CamSim already uses for scene sources: a fetch script plus published metrics, with the data never in the repo. No candidate raised an export-control flag on the data itself, apart from ESDC. Because the DSIAC ATR set is US Army collection material, the request form's eligibility terms, which were not seen, should be read before a foreign contributor is expected to reproduce results.

## Ranked recommendation: one flight, two benchmarks, one replay set

**Rank 1: a self-collected Camp Pendleton EO + LWIR set (primary test set).** No public set puts labelled vehicles under telemetry on the ground that CamSim's first scene package rebuilds, and none has military vehicles from a UAV view. A small collection fixes every mismatch at once. Fly a dual EO/radiometric-LWIR drone (the M30T or 640T class used by FLAME 3) along an altitude and pitch grid modelled on Archangel's metadata-stratified design. Archangel's real subset has per-image altitude, radius and viewing angle in 5 m steps and a matched synthetic twin ([arXiv 2209.00128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00128)), and follow-up papers use it as their real test set ([arXiv 2310.16255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16255)). Stage known vehicles, including tactical trucks if the base can provide them, along with civilian cars, and log their GPS. Fly at noon and at night, and over the shoreline for boats. Convert the drone video and flight record to MISB ST 0601 through the QGIS FMV route ([QGIS FMV](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/QGIS_FMV/version/4.0/)) and check it with `check.js`. Label in COCO with OBBs. Then replay every logged pose through CIGI into the Pendleton package to get pixel-paired synthetic frames. This is the only tier that answers "how close is CamSim at this exact place and pose", and the only one whose licence CamSim controls. A few hundred labelled frames per band and condition are enough for a fixed test set.

**Rank 2: VEDAI + HIT-UAV (+ SeaDronesSee) as the public comparability tier.** These give numbers that outsiders can reproduce. VEDAI is the closest US vehicle yardstick and matches CamSim's NAIP-based nadir look. HIT-UAV is the only CC BY aerial LWIR vehicle benchmark with per-image altitude and angle, so CamSim's own render can match its 60–130 m, 30–90° envelope even though the location cannot be matched. SeaDronesSee covers boats, with telemetry and a server-held test split that blocks accidental tuning on test labels. Report stratified by altitude and angle where the metadata allows. Keep all three behind download scripts.

**Rank 3: Caltech CART as the US pose-replay RGB-T set.** Use CART's GPS/IMU logs to build scene packages over two or three of its sites, such as the Castaic Lake or Joshua Tree flights, and replay its trajectories at night. Its masks support a segmentation comparison against CamSim's 4D semantic image, and its handful of cars and boats gives a small detection check. It is the best way to validate the IR pipeline on real US terrain before the Pendleton flight exists. Two near-misses deserve a mention. **MEVA UAV** is the fallback if a Pendleton flight is blocked: CC BY footage of a US military training site with camera models, which CamSim could label and publish. The **DSIAC ATR** database is worth requesting as a secondary MWIR signature check on real military vehicles, with its ground geometry flagged in any result.

### Open decisions for the project owner

**Pendleton access and release.** Will the base allow UAV flights and staged vehicles, and will its public affairs office clear imagery of the installation for publication? If not, the Rank 1 set stays internal, and the public story leans on MEVA, HIT-UAV and VEDAI. **Targets.** Should the vehicle set include real tactical vehicles (base-provided) or civilian stand-ins? Do boats belong in the first collection? **Thermal band.** Should Rank 1 use an uncooled LWIR drone camera, matching all public data, or try to borrow a cooled MWIR to match CamSim's default preset? **Licence policy.** Is CamSim content to report metrics on non-commercial benchmarks (VEDAI, CART, SDD) alongside its own licence, or should the public tier be restricted to CC BY/CC0 sets? **Budget.** How large should the labelling effort be, and should it be done in-house or contracted? **Three outreach requests**, each cheap: ask UCF for CitySim's US site coordinates, request DSIAC ATR access, and register on SDMS to inspect CSUAV headers for GPS/INS.

## Conclusion

The research turns the question around. Public data cannot provide a geo- and pose-matched real counterpart to CamSim's output; it can only provide comparability. The gap is sharpest exactly where CamSim differentiates itself: physically based MWIR, military vehicles from the air, and KLV telemetry. No open dataset combines them, so a well-documented Pendleton collection would be useful well beyond CamSim. Released under a permissive licence with its CamSim twin, it would be the first open, US, telemetry-tagged, paired real/synthetic EO-IR vehicle benchmark, filling the role Archangel fills for people.

Two technical implications follow for the validation design. First, nearly all usable real thermal is uncooled LWIR shown as 8-bit AGC'd frames, so the `lwir_uncooled` preset and the AGC path need the same scrutiny as the default MWIR model. FLAME 3's radiometric TIFFs are the only way to anchor absolute temperatures. Second, the old government collections (2003–2009) predate today's NAIP and 3DEP, so they cannot support pixel-level scene comparison. Recency matters as much as location when choosing a matched set.
