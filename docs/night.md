# Night sky and absolute light levels (ROADMAP 6A)

Night EO frames whose level and noise follow the real sky: the moon's position, phase and elevation, starlight and
airglow for the sim date, time and place, seen through a radiometric EO detector. 6B (image intensifier) and 6C (light
points) build on the same light levels. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-night-sky-6a-design.md`.

## What is modelled

| Source | Model | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | `ACesiumSunSky`'s directional light (CesiumSunSky keeps positioning it), intensity = top-of-atmosphere illuminance | 127.5 klx × (1 AU / r)², r from the ephemeris |
| Moon | a second directional light on `ACamSimEnvironment` at **atmosphere light index 1**, Krisciunas & Schaefer (1991); the dome's disc: lunar-Lambert photometry × the LROC albedo map, IAU orientation ("Moon disc") | I = 10^(−0.4 (3.84 + 0.026 \|α\| + 4·10⁻⁹ α⁴)) fc × 10.764 lux/fc × (384,400 km / d)²: full moon ≈ 0.31 lux TOA, ≈ 0.25 lux on the ground with the moon at 72° |
| Night floor | starlight + airglow + zodiacal light as a uniform sky radiance in the dome | `night_floor_lux` 0.002 lux on the ground (clear, moonless) |
| Stars | Yale Bright Star Catalogue (BSC5, ~9,100 stars to V ≈ 6.5) binned into a 4096 × 2048 equatorial texture | E = 10^(−0.4 (V + 13.98)) lux per star |

- **Ephemeris** (`Environment/Ephemeris.h`, `FEphemeris`): low-precision Meeus (sun ch. 25, moon ch. 47 with the
  main 60 terms, topocentric parallax ch. 40, phase ch. 48, sidereal time ch. 12), ΔT from the Espenak–Meeus
  polynomial. Held to JPL DE421 by `CamSim.Environment.Ephemeris.*` (sun ≤ 0.05°, moon ≤ 0.2°). Az/el are geometric
  (no refraction). The observer is the camera: the sky is re-applied once per sim second and whenever the camera moves
  more than 1 km (the clock can stand still: lockstep, a static CIGI time).
- **Illumination** (`FNightIllumination`): TOA sun and moon, Kasten–Young air mass with V-band extinction k = 0.172 for
  the expected ground illuminance (log line and tests), the floor radiance, Kasten & Czeplak cloud factor from CIGI
  Weather Control (`1 − 0.75 × cover^3.4`, 0.25 at 100 %). It dims the floor and the moon light by the same factor: the
  moon light's intensity is `MoonToaLux × cloud factor` (`MoonLightLux`) and the moon casts no cloud shadows, so an
  overcast moonlit night is uniformly darker, not patchy. The dome's moon disc keeps its TOA luminance (the volumetric
  clouds, when drawn, cover it). `scripts/sky/ephemeris.py` mirrors both for the acceptance script.
- **Units: k = 1.** Scene colour / PreExposure is luminance in cd/m² and light intensities are lux. CesiumSunSky's
  `SkyAtmosphere->TransmittanceMinLightElevationAngle` is set back to −90 (Cesium sets 90, so the ground transmittance
  was computed as if the sun were at the zenith): sun and moon dim and redden with air mass and go dark below the
  horizon.

## UE setup

- **Moon light**: `UDirectionalLightComponent` with `bAtmosphereSunLight` and `AtmosphereSunLightIndex 1`, forward
  shading priority below the sun, 0.52° source angle, ~4,100 K tint, shadows (VSM) only while it is the brighter
  light; hidden with intensity 0 below the horizon. Its engine disc is black (`AtmosphereSunDiskColorScale`): the dome
  draws the phase.
- **Sky dome** `M_SkyDome` + `MPC_Sky` (Unlit, Opaque, **Is Sky**; generated by `scripts/sky/make_sky_material.sh`,
  never hand-edited): any IsSky mesh replaces the atmosphere in UE's real-time sky capture, so the dome draws the
  atmosphere itself (`SkyAtmosphereViewLuminance`) plus the sun disc, the moon disc (lunar-Lambert × albedo map,
  normalised so the disc integrates to the moon's illuminance: "Moon disc" below), the stars (point-sampled, rotated by local
  sidereal time, latitude and J2000 precession) and the floor; moon disc, stars and floor are attenuated by T(view
  elevation), computed once in one Custom node. The moon disc is left out of the real-time sky capture (UE's
  `RenderingReflectionCaptureMask`, as its own sun disc is): the moon directional light already lights the scene, and a
  captured 0.5° disc would add up to ~80 % of its light again through the sky light, depending on where it fell on the
  capture's ~0.7° texels.
  The floor radiance is `night_floor_lux / (π × 0.755)`, 0.755 being the cosine-weighted mean of T over the sky, so it
  delivers `night_floor_lux` on a horizontal surface.
- **Lumen cached-lighting band**: Lumen's surface cache and the sky capture store luminance × 2^−c in R11G11B10
  (c = `r.EyeAdaptation.CachedLightingPreExposure`); with c = 8 night lighting underflows. `FCachedExposureBand`
  switches c between `day_ev` 8 and `night_ev` −6 on the EO AE's scene median (night below 2^0 cd/m², back to day
  above 2^2). A switch makes UE drop the Lumen surface cache; for 15 frames after it the real-time sky capture runs
  whole-cubemap (`r.SkyLight.RealTimeReflectionCapture.TimeSlice 0`, `FSkyRecaptureHold`), otherwise the time-sliced
  capture kept a cubemap encoded at the old c for ~12 frames and the frame mean dipped ~50 DN for 1.5 s. In IR the band
  holds.
- **UE pre-exposure at night**: the view's `AutoExposureBias` follows the AE's target without the gain cap
  (`FSensorController::GetSceneExposureEv`, at most 15 EV above the gain), so night scene colour sits near target grey
  instead of fp16's floor. By day it is the sensor gain, as before. The sensor graph divides PreExposure out. The boost
  is capped at `FNightIllumination::SceneExposureCapEv` (1.39 EV): the brightest moon disc (full, perigee, zenith,
  ~6,250 cd/m²) then reaches 16384 in scene colour, a quarter of fp16's max, so a small moon over dark ground in a wide
  view can't overflow into Inf/NaN through TSR (N3's 40° view).
- **Moonlit clouds only at night**: the moon light's `CloudScatteredLuminanceScale` is white while the moon is up and the
  sun below the horizon, black otherwise (UE's second cloud light costs ~0.4 ms GPU; by day it lit nothing).

## Moon disc

The dome draws the moon as the real one looks: nearly uniform to the limb at full moon, with maria, and the phase's
terminator on the sun's side (it was a Lambert ball, darkening smoothly to the limb).

- **Photometry**: the lunar-Lambert law (McEwen 1991, *Icarus* 92, 298), r ∝ 2L μ₀/(μ₀ + μ) + (1 − L) μ₀, μ₀ / μ the
  cosines of incidence and emission, with McEwen's (1996, LPSC XXVII, 841) phase polynomial
  L(α) = 1 − 0.019 α + 2.42·10⁻⁴ α² − 1.46·10⁻⁶ α³ (α in degrees, clamped to [0, 1]): L = 1 at full moon is the
  Lommel–Seeliger law (regolith: uniform brightness to the limb), falling toward Lambert as the phase grows
  (`FMoonDisc::LunarLambertL`, `Photometric`).
  The polynomial reaches 0 near 110° and is clamped there: crescents past ~110° are pure Lambert (μ₀). At those phases
  K&S has the moon at < 4 % of full, and the crescent's shape is set by the terminator more than by the limb law.
- **Albedo**: `Content/NonUFS/Sky/moon_albedo.pgm` (git LFS, 1024 × 512, 8-bit linear relative albedo) from the NASA
  SVS CGI Moon Kit colour map (2025 version, adapted from the LROC WAC Hapke-normalised mosaic: 643/566/415 nm),
  linearised, Rec. 709 luminance, 4 × 4 block mean. NASA imagery, public domain (credit NASA's Scientific
  Visualization Studio, NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University). `scripts/sky/fetch_moon_albedo.sh` rebuilds it from a
  pinned URL and sha256s; `Content/NonUFS/Sky/README.md` has the provenance. The map's mare / highland ratio is ~2.5
  (Hapke-normalised 643 nm reflectance; visual-band normal albedos are nearer 2): kept as is. Missing or an LFS
  pointer: one warning and a uniform disc. The GPU copy is a transient linear BGRA8 texture with a box-filtered mip
  chain (G8, 0.7 MB); `M_SkyDome` samples it at an explicit LOD from the view direction's derivatives (no aliasing in
  wide views). `CamSim.GPU.Sky.MoonDiscMatchesCpu` renders the dome's disc and holds it to `FMoonDisc::Radiance` (0.04 %
  rms, absolute level within 0.04 %); `CamSim.Environment.MoonSurface.OrientationOnSky` catches a mirrored map or a
  rotation sign error.
- **Orientation**: the moon's body axes from the IAU WGCCRE 2009 rotation model (Archinal et al. 2011, *Celest. Mech.
  Dyn. Astron.* 109, 101: pole and prime meridian with the E1–E13 periodic terms; NAIF `pck00010` BODY301), the
  mean Earth / polar axis frame of the LROC maps. Optical libration (the observer's real direction, topocentric) and
  the physical libration in the periodic terms follow; held to Meeus ex. 53.a (l, b to 0.03°, the axis's position
  angle to 0.04°) by `CamSim.Environment.MoonSurface.IauOrientationMeeus53a`. `MPC_Sky` carries the axes in UE world
  (`MoonAxisX/Y/Z`) and L in `MoonParams.z`.
- **Normalisation**: `FSkyDomeDriver` scales the disc by E / (Ω × mean), the mean being the albedo × lunar-Lambert mean
  over the disc for the current phase and libration (`FMoonDisc::MeanRadiance`, the CPU mirror of `CamSimMoonDisc`,
  a 128² grid, 256² past phase 100°, ~0.33 ms; `FMoonNormaliserCache` recomputes it only when the phase, the
  sub-observer or the sub-solar point moved > 0.05°, about once per 6 wall seconds at 60× sim time): the disc still integrates to the K&S illuminance (within 1 % at phases 0–135°,
  `CamSim.Environment.MoonSurface.Normaliser`); the moon light and N2 are unchanged. The pre-exposure cap uses the
  map's full-moon peak-to-mean bound `FMoonDisc::MaxPeakToMean` 2.75 (2.54 measured over librations; the full-moon K&S
  value bounds the other phases): the brightest disc
  point is ~11,500 cd/m², `SceneExposureCapEv` 0.52 EV.
- With the sun exactly on the line of sight (eclipse, conjunction) the disc frame picks any perpendicular
  (`CamSimMoonRight`): no NaN, the shading is symmetric there.

## Twilight colour

UE's SkyAtmosphere default ozone ("other absorption", 0.650 / 1.881 / 0.085 · 10⁻³ km⁻¹ at the 25 km tent peak) is
Bruneton's (2017) cross section sampled at 680, 550 and 440 nm. That puts the Chappuis band's peak (~600 nm) in the green
channel (G/R 2.9). By day the ozone column is thin and it hardly matters, but in twilight the light reaching the sky
has crossed the ozone layer at grazing incidence, and the green-heavy filter turned the sky, and the ground under it,
magenta: at sun −3° the zenith had R/G 1.6 and the ground's illuminant was 1.6× redder and 1.9× bluer than daylight
relative to green (pre-6A too: it is the engine default). The real twilight sky is blue because ozone's Chappuis band
absorbs the long wavelengths (Spitschan et al. 2016, *Sci. Rep.* 6, 26756: the illuminant grows bluer from sunset to
nautical twilight, "Chappuis filtering").

`ACamSimEnvironment` sets the ozone to its linear-sRGB band integral instead (`FNightIllumination::OzoneAbsorptionPerKm`,
generated by `scripts/sky/ozone_rgb.py`: Bruneton's cross sections, CIE 1931 observer, IEC 61966-2-1 matrix, a
5,778 K sun, each channel fitted by exp(−k p) over 0.5–300 km of peak-density path): **2.46 / 1.77 / 0 · 10⁻³ km⁻¹**
(the same column at green; red absorbed most). Root cause found by experiment (control API, Pendleton slant 500 m
and the zenith at sun −3° and −6°): the TransmittanceMinLightElevationAngle change, the dome (hidden), the floor, the
moon light and the sky light's intensity changed nothing or only the level; ozone off made the twilight sky grey-yellow
(Hulburt's no-ozone sky); the band-integrated ozone made it blue. Day colour changes slightly (N1 below).

Ozone fit caveats: the fit weights a 5,778 K blackbody sun (a D65 weighting gives 2.55 / 1.75 / 0.02, no visible
difference), and it fits ozone alone. Light that crossed a long air path before the ozone layer has had its blue and
green scattered out, which shifts the red channel's energy past the Chappuis peak; fitted after 160–300 km of Rayleigh
path, k_R ≈ 1.9–2.1 and k_G ≈ 1.9–2.3 · 10⁻³ km⁻¹. One set of RGB coefficients cannot be right for both, so
near-horizon light loses 15–25 % too much R/G over its ~250 km ozone path. This is a limit of three-channel transport.

**Measured reference and the fix-round findings (2026-10-17).** `scripts/sky/twilight_bands.py` turns the
supplementary spectra of Spitschan et al. (2016) into illuminant bands: rural site, downwelling irradiance, one spectrum
a minute, June–July 2014. It computes linear sRGB R/G and B/G relative to the 20–26° spectra and writes the 10/50/90 %
percentiles per sun elevation to `twilight_bands.json`:

| Sun | R/G (10–50–90 %) | B/G | CCT (by R/G / by B/G, median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| +3° | 0.67 – 0.79 – 0.88 | 1.11 – 1.23 – 1.45 | 10,200 K / 7,800 K |
| 0° | 0.40 – 0.74 – 0.82 | 1.16 – 1.44 – 2.10 | 12,900 K / 9,900 K |
| −3° | 0.27 – 0.47 – 0.78 | 1.28 – 1.97 – 2.66 | past the locus / 25,000 K |
| −6° | 0.22 – 0.35 – 0.66 | 1.74 – 2.34 – 3.05 | past the locus |

The real horizontal illuminant at sunset is about 10,000 K, not warm. Its R/G and B/G imply different temperatures:
it lies off the Planckian locus toward cyan by about 30 mired. CamSim's 0° ground matches this (R/G ×0.70,
B/G ×1.56, nadir 100 m). The "teal" ground at sun 0° is real.

Experiments at +3/0/−3/−6° covered: ozone off, R = G, Rayleigh-coupled, UE's default and ×1.3; the sky light's lower
hemisphere; the dome hidden; floor and moon off; Lumen off; multiple scattering off; Mie off; the planet's ground albedo
0.1; and band-integrated Rayleigh. One real defect came out of them. Cesium's sky light keeps its lower hemisphere
(`bLowerHemisphereIsBlack` false), and the real-time capture's lower half is SkyAtmosphere's planet surface lit by the
reddened sun. Through Lumen's distant lighting and the sky light SH, that light reached the horizontal ground, and the
effect depended on the view (nadir 100 m, sun −6°: R/G ×1.35, slant 500 m: ×0.91). CamSim now sets the lower
hemisphere black, UE's default (×1.05; 0° ×0.82 → ×0.70; day unchanged).

**Limit.** UE's RGB atmosphere keeps too much red at +3°, −3° and −6°: R/G ×1.04, ×0.88 and ×1.05 against the measured
×0.79, ×0.47 and ×0.35. B/G is inside the measured bands at every elevation, so the illuminant sits off the locus toward
magenta by 45–115 mired. No physical parameter moved R/G at −3° or −6° by more than ~0.1 except UE's green-heavy
default ozone, which moves it the wrong way. At those sun elevations the zenith is lit by light that crossed the
atmosphere above most of UE's ozone tent (10–40 km). Matching would need spectral, or at least more than three-band,
transport. N9 gates what CamSim matches (0° in band, B/G everywhere, the colour temperature rising with depression)
and reports R/G at +3/−3/−6° as this limit.

## Radiometric EO exposure (ROADMAP 9.4, folded into 6A)

For a photon detector with lens transmission and QE set (the `eo_hd_cmos` preset), the photon gain is an integration
time (`Sensor/Radiometry.h`):

```
R          = π / (4 N²) · τ · A_pix · QE · photons_per_lm        [electrons / s per cd/m²]
PhotonGain = R · t_int / FullWellE
```

`eo_hd_cmos`: N = 4, pixel pitch 2.9 µm, τ = 0.9, QE = 0.6, photons per lumen of a 5,800 K source over 0.4–0.7 µm
≈ 1.09·10¹⁶ (computed at load), full well 10,000 e: **R ≈ 2,440 e/s per cd/m²**. The AE's photon stage spans
t_int = `integration_min_us` 10 µs … `integration_max_ms` 33.3 ms, i.e. −18.6 … **−6.94 EV**; past t_max it adds analog
gain up to 30 dB (total cap −1.96 EV). The old calibrated EO cap was −12.5 EV, so dusk and night now integrate up to
5.6 EV longer before analog gain; daylight (AE below the cap) is unchanged by construction (N1). Dark signal integrates
over t_int. `exposure.min_gain_ev` / `max_photon_gain_ev` on a radiometric mode log one warning and are ignored. IR
keeps its calibrated `thermal_exposure` (9.4's IR part stays in Milestone 9). The AE reads its median and highlight
percentiles interpolated inside the 1/8-EV histogram bins (whole bins stepped the gain 1/8 EV at a time, a 3.6 DN
sawtooth through dusk).

What a full-moon night looks like at the defaults (slant 500 m, Camp Pendleton): scene median 2^−4.4 cd/m², t_int at
33.3 ms and 30 dB analog gain, frame mean Y ≈ 28 DN, 4.7 DN temporal noise on a flat patch; moonless: median 2^−11.7,
mean Y ≈ 19 DN, read-noise dominated (2.9 DN). Stars to V ≈ 6 need a longer integration than a 30 fps camera has: `night_check.py` N4 uses
`detector.integration_max_ms: 2000`.

## CIGI

Celestial Sphere Control: Sun / Moon / Star Field Enable zero that source (light and disc); Star Field Intensity is a
percent (100 = physical); Ephemeris Model Enable keeps driving the clock's rate; an enable- or intensity-only change
re-applies the sky without moving the clock. Until the first packet, `stars_default` (true) decides the stars (CIGI's
own default is off). `scripts/send_cigi_test.py --time HHMM` sends Sun, Moon and Star Field Enable with Star Field
Intensity 100 % by default (`--stars PCT`; `--stars 0` sends the star field off), so the reference host shows the 6A
sky; the bench (`scripts/bench/scenario.py`) uses the same packet. The HITL host sends 100 % too. `scripts/cigi_web_ui.py`
still sends the star field off (and packs the Date field at the wrong offset: a pre-existing bug, not 6A's). CIGI
visibility does nothing with `Main.umap` (no `ExponentialHeightFog`; ROADMAP 8.6).

## Limits

- A star is one texel (≈ 5′): ~2.8 px at 40° FOV, a small square at narrow FOVs; no scintillation.
- No earthshine on the dark limb, no planets, no Milky Way as an extended source; opposition surge only as K&S.
- Exponential height fog's inscattering uses light 0 only (and Main.umap has no fog actor).
- Thermal IR's class model (`FThermalModel`) still takes its sun from `ACamSimEnvironment::ComputeSunPosition`, not
  `FEphemeris` (a follow-up: it changes the thermal calibration). The rest of thermal's sun is 6A's: the solar input's
  gate and the telemetry / KLV sun elevation (`GetSunElevationDeg`) come from `FEphemeris`, and the per-pixel solar
  term reads the 127.5 klx TOA sun light through UE's transmittance, which now includes the slant path (N6 passes;
  `docs/thermal.md`).
- No lunar eclipse: the moon keeps its K&S brightness through totality.
- The moon disc is grey (the light has the ~4,100 K tint, the disc doesn't). The albedo map has 0.35° (10.7 km) texels:
  at 2° FOV the disc centre is ~1 texel per pixel, so narrower views magnify it (soft).
- UE's pre-exposure cap (`SceneExposureCapEv`, 1.39 EV) is a constant set for the brightest full moon: with the moon
  down the dark-scene boost is ~7.6 EV less than the AE target, so new-moon deep shadows sit near 2^−13 in scene colour
  again (ROADMAP 6A open items).
- Overcast dims the moon light uniformly by the floor's cloud factor (0.25 at full cover), not by cloud shadows, so it
  also dims the moonlit tops of the volumetric clouds seen from above; no gate images moonlit clouds.
- `M_SkyDome` has not been compiled or run on Metal yet; the M1 Pro run of `night_check.py` is open.
- The star texture keeps a 64 MB CPU copy and 64 MB of VRAM, and `FStarField::Bin` allocates a ~200 MB transient
  accumulator at startup (capacity is VRAM-bound: `docs/capacity-linux-rtx5080.md`).
- `r.Test.FreezeTemporalSequences 1` (the R6 determinism pin) makes TSR hold stale light after a large lighting jump
  (full → new moon, night → noon in lockstep): `night_check.py` leaves it off for its night runs.

## Acceptance

`scripts/night_check.py --scene PKG --out DIR [--runs day,moon,stars,dusk,thermal,cost] [--baseline DIR]` (gates
N1–N9, spec table). Each gate writes `DIR/<gate>.json` (measured values, threshold, pass/fail, UTC time and commit)
and every invocation rewrites `summary.md` with all nine gates: this invocation's plus the `N*.json` earlier ones
left in `DIR` (marked "earlier invocation"), so runs can be split over several calls. The baseline is the same script's `--runs day,cost` on the pre-6A commit `59c3ff6` (a worktree built with
its own `scripts/run.sh --build-only`; copy `night_check.py`, `scripts/sky/ephemeris.py` and the bench's
`scenario.py` / `run_bench.py` into it first).

```bash
uv run --project scripts/control --with numpy --with pillow --with scipy --with skyfield \
    python scripts/night_check.py --scene .cache/scene-packages/pendleton-r4 --out .cache/night/6a \
    --baseline .cache/night/base
```

How each gate is measured (all in `scripts/night_check.py`): Y is the NV12 luma recovered from the capture PNG; temporal
noise is std(frame a − frame b) / √2 over the flattest 64 × 64 block. N2's mirror runs `CamSimSensorRef::Run`'s steps
for a uniform field (PRNU, dark, shot, DSNU, read noise, full-well clip, analog gain, ADC, knee + BT.709, NV12 packing,
the PNG's BGRA conversion) at the logged photon/analog gain and t_int, with the per-channel level fitted to the patch's
mean RGB. N3's model is the dome's disc (`scripts/sky/moon_surface.py`, the mirror of `FMoonDisc::Radiance`: albedo map, IAU
axes, lunar-Lambert) at the moon direction CamSim logged (`camsim.Sky.Log`, 10⁻⁵°; the Python ephemeris mirror is
11″ off at the full-moon pose, 2 px at 2°), through the display curve fitted to the frame; the disc offset is a
least-squares registration of the frame on it (± 6 px, parabolic sub-pixel). Limb / centre and std / mean are on the
frame's linear signal (BT.709 OETF and knee inverted). N9 takes the mean linear RGB of a 40° nadir view from 100 m and of a 30° view 85° up, away from the sun, from lockstep
captures after the dusk recording at sun +22, +3, 0, −3 and −6°. It turns each ratio into the colour temperature of
the blackbody with that ratio relative to the measured 6,130 K reference (`twilight_bands.json`'s table). N4 detects on the mean of two frames (blobs ≥ 2 px: a hot pixel
is one) and projects BSC5 with skyfield. N5/N8 record the raw MPEG-TS (never through ffmpeg: KLV), time each frame by its
ST 0601 Tag 2 and take the band switch from the log; N8 smooths the mean with a 15-frame median before the monotonic
test.

**N7 cost.** The night bench pose has a half moon at 36° (brighter than the sun at −11°), so the moon light casts VSM
shadows and, as atmosphere light 1, lights the level's volumetric clouds, which UE treats as an expensive second cloud
light. Measured at that pose over the control API (40 s, GPU p50): clouds and moon on 3.52 ms, moon off 3.01 ms, moon
on without cloud scattering 3.10 ms; clouds off 2.67 vs 2.52 ms. The moonlit clouds cost ~0.4 ms and the moon's
shadows ~0.1 ms at night; the spec's N7 was amended (2026-10-16) to allow +0.6 ms GPU with the moon up, and by day the
moon no longer lights the clouds. A band switch also costs 15 frames of whole-cubemap sky capture (a short GPU spike once
per dusk or dawn, not in the steady-state bench).
